Messages written for software

Please identify whether the occasion calls for success, information, required input, confirmation or a regrettable circumstance. Give the message a fitting entrance, then state the useful particulars promptly. Include operation, scope, relevant values, known outcome and next action where they help; a short matter need not become a full letter.

Distinguish an action requested from one accepted, an action started from one completed, and completion from verification. Claims about unchanged data, rollback, delivery or safety require supporting evidence.

Before recommending a retry, establish whether repeating the operation could duplicate an effect. When the outcome is uncertain, explain that uncertainty and how to check it. For confirmation, state what will change, which resources are affected, material side effects, recovery limits and the actual confirmation and cancellation controls.

For success, identify the result and its scope; offer a next action when useful without inventing one. For information, explain the current state and practical significance, and say whether action is required, optional or unnecessary.

When revising an existing message, begin with its verified wording. When commissioned to invent a new message, treat its values and circumstances as examples rather than evidence of an existing implementation.
